## Corrected force accounting

Previously, contact producer timestamps up to 100 ms ahead of the ROS clock were clamped to that clock. Several sequential physics samples could therefore collapse into one timestamp and have their forces added. The [manipulation node](../src/erc_phase1_solution/erc_phase1_solution/manipulation_node.py) now:

- preserves contact producer timestamps and keeps the newest 64 frames in timestamp order;
- counts each collision pair once per timestamp, retaining the larger duplicate value, while summing distinct contacting finger parts;
- waits at most 0.5 wall seconds for the ROS clock to reach a fixed snapshot within the existing 100 ms skew bound, without rewriting timestamps or chasing later frames;
- retains stale, malformed, future-data, identity and overload rejection, including overloads arriving during that wait.

A later concurrency correction copies both force histories **and gripper feedback before reading the evaluation clock**. The fixed catch-up timestamp includes the newest contact or feedback stamp, and the evaluator uses that same copied feedback after the bounded wait. A newer JointState callback can no longer replace it after the clock was read and create a false future-data rejection. The 100 ms skew bound, 0.5 s maximum wait and overload/cancellation checks remain unchanged.

The 8 N overload threshold and 1 mm closing steps are unchanged. Per-side `force_peaks` diagnostics now retain the peak magnitude, producer timestamp, book model and observed gripper joint position through confirmation-window resets. The position is contemporaneous callback feedback, not a hardware-synchronized force measurement.

The installed Gazebo 8.15 Contact system publishes active contacts every physics step: **500 Hz with the official 2 ms timestep**, despite the URDF sensor's 30 Hz tag. It does not publish empty contact messages. Gazebo supplies one contact record per collision pair and one wrench per contact point, with equal/opposite forces on the two bodies. The solution selects only the gripper body's force and sums point magnitudes; this is conservative contact load, not net vector force. [Contact system](https://github.com/gazebosim/gz-sim/blob/gz-sim8_8.15.0/src/systems/contact/Contact.cc), [physics implementation](https://github.com/gazebosim/gz-sim/blob/gz-sim8_8.15.0/src/systems/physics/Physics.cc).
